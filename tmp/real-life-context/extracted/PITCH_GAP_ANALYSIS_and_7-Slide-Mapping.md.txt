# PITCH DEVELOPMENT: Gap Analysis + 7-Slide Mapping
**Real-Life Challenges for BeReal**

---

## 🔴 THE GAP: Where Direction Changed

### Projects 1-2 ORIGINAL Direction
**Problem:** Millennials (28+) experience cognitive overload and mechanic opacity in BeReal's posting flow
**Root Cause:** Dual-camera + post-to-unlock mechanics are confusing
**Solution:** Add contextual cues and help text at friction-heavy moments (UX refinement)
**Goal:** Clearer UI → more consistent posting → reduced millennial churn

### Projects 3-4 FINAL Direction (Your Reframe)
**Problem:** BeReal lacks a compelling invitation reason. No one has a good reason to ask friends to join. The app stays empty.
**Root Cause:** Invitation pitch "see my photos" doesn't work for anyone (millennials or Gen Z)
**Solution:** Real-Life Challenges creates shared actions as the real reason to invite
**Goal:** Shared purpose → friends download and stay → network effects compound

### The Pivot Point
**Between Project 2 and Project 3**, the diagnosis changed:
- Project 2 revealed: "The existing mechanics actually work fine. But millennials still leave. Why?"
- Answer: It's not the UI. It's the network and purpose.
- Realization: Can't fix a network problem with clearer help text. Need a feature that gives people a reason to *build* their network.

### Discard These Elements

## 🎯 7-SLIDE PITCH MAPPING: What Goes Where

### SLIDE 1: THE PROBLEM

**Visual:** Empty BeReal feed (screenshot from Project 1)

**Key Talking Points:**
- "BeReal peaked at 73.5M users in 2022. Today it's 40M. Downloads fell 93.5M → ~6M." (Project 1 data)
- "But here's the real problem: When you ask a friend to join, what's your pitch?"
- "' Download so you can see my cereal bowl and my face at 9 AM'? That doesn't work."
- "That's the empty room problem. No one has a compelling reason to invite friends."

**Source Projects:** Project 1 (data), Your Reframe (insight)

**Direction Shift:** Don't say "millennials confused by mechanics." Say "anyone trying to invite friends has no good reason." (Broader, more strategic)

---

### SLIDE 2: WHAT WE LEARNED (Insights)

**Visual:** Focus group quotes (from Project 4)

**Key Talking Points:**
- "Users want authentic connection, not performance pressure." (Project 2 finding)
- "Gen Z specifically cares about wellness and mental health, but not fitness app vibes." (Project 1 research)
- "And this is critical: When we showed users a simple iMessage invite — 'Do a challenge with me' — all 5 instantly said yes and named someone." (Project 4)
- "The insight: People don't need clearer mechanics. They need PURPOSE."

**Source Projects:** Project 2 (retention insight), Project 4 (focus group validation), Project 1 (Gen Z trends)

**Direction Shift:** Flip from "Users need UI help" to "Users need PURPOSE and CONNECTION"

---

### SLIDE 3: THE SOLUTION — Real-Life Challenges

**Visual:** Prototype screenshot (Project 3, Screen 02 — categories) or Canva mockup

**Key Talking Points:**
- "Real-Life Challenges is not a fitness app. It's five types of small shared actions: Movement, Mood, Care, Connection, Outside."
- "You and a close friend make a tiny commitment. Drink water today. Take a walk. Send a voice note."
- "Then you prove it the way BeReal already does — with a real moment. Photo, voice note, or check-in."
- "No scores. No streaks. No performance pressure. Just two people showing up for something together."

**Source Projects:** Project 3 (feature design), Project 4 (validation it feels native to BeReal)

**Direction Shift:** Entirely new direction. Projects 1-2 had no alternative solution; Project 3 built it.

---

### SLIDE 4: HOW IT WORKS / Prototype

**Visual:** 3-step flow diagram or prototype walkthrough (Project 3)

**Key Talking Points:**
- "Step 1: You invite a friend via iMessage. 'I started a Real-Life Challenge with you. Drink water today. Small challenge, no score.'"
- "Step 2: They download BeReal. They do the thing. You both capture proof."
- "Step 3: After a few days, you have a private moment vault together. Not a feed. Not a leaderboard. A small album."

**Source Projects:** Project 3 (6-screen MVP design), Project 4 (iMessage invite tested and validated)

**Direction Shift:** Projects 1-2 tried to fix existing flow; Project 3 designed entirely new flow.

---

### SLIDE 5: USER FEEDBACK & EVIDENCE

**Visual:** Quote card + stat (from Project 4 focus group)

**Key Talking Points:**
- "We tested this with 5 people: 3 daily BeReal users, 2 who'd never tried it."
- "Result: All 5 instantly named a specific person they'd do a challenge with."
- "Tomás (daily user): 'No streaks changed everything. Lost a 200-day Duolingo streak and never opened again. This is why I'd try Real-Life Challenges.'"
- "Miriam (non-user): 'I'd download for my friend in Lagos immediately. Wouldn't download just to see photos.'"
- "Key validation: The iMessage invitation worked as a standalone pitch — even for non-users."

**Source Projects:** Project 4 (focus group methodology, findings, quotes)

**Direction Shift:** Projects 1-2 had no validation. Project 4 provides concept validation with real users.

---

### SLIDE 6: THE IMPACT

**Visual:** Simple metric or projection (from Project 3)

**Key Talking Points:**
- "Primary metric: Weekly active participation in Real-Life Challenges."
- "Why this matters: Right now, users open BeReal for one moment, then close. With Real-Life Challenges, they have a reason to return daily for 3-7 days to complete a shared ritual."
- "Secondary impact: Every friend you invite who downloads the app = network expansion. Every friend who completes a challenge = habit formation."
- "Potential: 30-day retention lift for challenge participants vs. non-participants."

**Source Projects:** Project 3 (metrics framework), Project 1 (commercial case for why millennial expansion matters)

**Direction Shift:** Projects 1-2 focused on "reduce churn." Project 3 focuses on "create network effects." Much bigger opportunity.

---

### SLIDE 7: RECOMMENDATION / NEXT STEPS

**Visual:** Simple roadmap or action items

**Key Talking Points:**
- "The concept is validated. Prototype is tested. Focus group proved the iMessage invitation works."
- "Recommendation: Proceed to expanded testing. We need 8-10 more participants, including more non-users and people 35+."
- "Critical design work: The 'missed day' screen (what happens when someone doesn't participate — this is the trust-building moment)."
- "Copy refinement: Test invitation variants for users outside BeReal."
- "Timeline: 2-week testing → design refinement → 4-week beta pilot."

**Source Projects:** Project 3 (RICE analysis, next steps), Project 4 (next steps recommendation)

**Direction Shift:** Projects 1-2 recommended UX tweaks (wrong direction). Project 3-4 recommend expand testing (right direction).
