[Normal] PRD-lite: Real-Life Challenges
[Normal] Small real-life pacts that help close friends show up for themselves and each other.
[Heading 1] 1. Problem Statement
[Normal] BeReal is good at capturing one spontaneous daily moment, but it does not yet help users turn real-life actions into shared rituals with close friends. Users who want more connection, wellness, or accountability may not have a lightweight way to share small meaningful actions without leaving BeReal for fitness, habit, or messaging apps.
[Heading 1] 2. Target User
[Normal] The target user is a Gen Z or young millennial BeReal user who values authenticity, close-friend connection, and small daily routines. This user does not want a performance-based fitness app, but may enjoy doing simple real-life pacts with a best friend, partner, sibling, or small group.
[Heading 1] 3. Success Metric
[Normal] Primary Metric: Weekly active participation in Real-Life Challenges.
[Normal] This connects to MAU because the feature gives users more meaningful reasons to return during the month, not only when the daily BeReal notification appears.
[Heading 1] 4. Assumptions and Non-Goals
[Heading 2] Assumptions
[Normal] • Users want more reasons to open BeReal beyond the daily post.
[Normal] • Close-friend accountability can make small actions feel more fun and meaningful.
[Normal] • Gen Z and young millennials are interested in wellness, but they do not necessarily want another fitness or productivity app.
[Normal] • Lightweight proof formats like photos, short videos, voice notes, mood words, and simple check-ins can make the feature feel native to BeReal.
[Normal] • The feature must feel playful and human, not like a leaderboard or optimization tool.
[Heading 2] Non-Goals
[Normal] • Build a fitness app or compete directly with Strava, Apple Fitness, or Fitbit.
[Normal] • Create public leaderboards by default.
[Normal] • Share sensitive health, mood, sleep, medication, or location data by default.
[Normal] • Add filters, beauty tools, or curated feeds.
[Normal] • Become a mental health treatment tool.
[Normal] • Build full real-time chat.
[Heading 1] 5. Guardrails for BeReal’s Brand
[Heading 1] Evidence Notes
[Normal] • BeReal publicly describes itself as a simple app where friends post once a day to show what they are really doing, with no filters and no influencer-style pressure.
[Normal] • BeReal reports over 40 million monthly active users and says 85% of its users are Gen Z, which makes Gen Z a core audience for the feature.
[Normal] • McKinsey reports that younger consumers, especially Gen Z and millennials, are actively prioritizing wellness areas such as fitness, mindfulness, and mental health.
[Normal] • Gallup reports that adults aged 18 to 34 in the U.S. are drinking less than young adults in prior decades, supporting a broader shift toward more intentional health behavior.
[Normal] • Academic research on BeReal found that its design supports spontaneous sharing, reciprocal posting, curated audiences, and lower self-presentation pressure, but can also feel limited. This supports expanding expression carefully without making BeReal performative.

TABLE 1
Feature framing This is not a fitness app, a habit tracker, or a public leaderboard. The idea is to expand BeReal from one spontaneous daily post into small shared rituals that still feel authentic, simple, private, and human.

TABLE 2
Secondary Metric | Why it matters
Challenge creation rate | Shows whether users understand and want the feature.
Challenge completion rate | Shows whether the ritual is actually being used.
Challenge posts per user per week | Measures new posting behavior beyond the daily BeReal.
Friend response rate | Measures whether it creates connection, not only self-tracking.
30-day retention comparison | Compares challenge users with non-challenge users.
Repeat challenge rate | Shows whether users want to continue after the first challenge.

TABLE 3
Guardrail | How the feature should follow it
Authenticity | Challenges should be based on real actions and real proof, not polished content.
Privacy | Users should control who sees each challenge, and sensitive data should always be optional.
Simplicity | A challenge should be easy to create and complete in a few steps.
Positive Behavior | The feature should encourage connection and showing up, not shame or comparison.
BeReal Fit | Real-Life Challenges should expand BeReal’s real-life sharing without turning it into Strava, a habit tracker, or a productivity app.
