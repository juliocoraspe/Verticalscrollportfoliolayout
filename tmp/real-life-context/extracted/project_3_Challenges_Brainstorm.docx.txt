[Normal] BeReal Real-Life Challenges
[Normal] Brainstorming Notes and Product Concept
[Heading 1] 1. Concept Summary
[Normal] Real-Life Challenges are not fitness challenges in the traditional sense. They are small real-life pacts between close friends, partners, or small groups.
[Normal] The goal is to make BeReal more playful and human by turning small actions into shared rituals. The feature should feel like presence, not performance.
[Normal] This direction connects wellness, movement, mood, daily care, and human connection without turning BeReal into Strava, Apple Fitness, or a mental health treatment app.
[Heading 1] 2. Main Product Insight
[Normal] For BeReal, the opportunity is not to track performance. The opportunity is to help people share proof of real life with the people who matter.

[Heading 1] 8. Suggested User Flow
[List Number] Pick a Pact: choose something real to do together.
[List Number] Choose the person or small group.
[List Number] Choose the proof type: photo, dual-camera, short video, voice note, mood word, or simple check-in.
[List Number] Set the rhythm: today, 3 days, 7 days, weekend, or weekly.
[List Number] Receive a light notification when it is time to show up.
[List Number] Post proof. The other person sees it after they complete theirs, keeping reciprocity.
[List Number] React or reply.
[List Number] See a small recap at the end of the challenge.
[Heading 1] 9. Competitive Layer Without Becoming Toxic
[Normal] Care Mode is important because a user who is struggling should not feel punished by the product. Language should be gentle: “Want to restart today?” instead of “You failed.”
[Heading 1] 10. Connection With the Widget
[Normal] The widget can become the soft presence layer for the challenge system. Instead of only showing a photo, it can show the relationship ritual in progress.

[Heading 1] 13. Strategic Positioning
[Normal] This feature is stronger than a simple BeReal widget because it creates its own reason to exist. The widget can support it later, but the deeper idea is the ritual itself.
[Normal] It is also more original than a pure UI clarity layer. Clarity Layer may score higher in RICE, but it could conflict with deliberate design decisions already made by BeReal. Real-Life Challenges offers a new canvas while staying close to the platform’s values.
[Normal] The final direction should be playful and human: not fun because of extra emojis or motion, but fun because people enjoy showing up, competing lightly, and sharing the journey with someone they care about.

TABLE 1
Core idea A social ritual engine for real life: small challenges that help close friends show up for themselves and each other through photos, video, voice notes, simple check-ins, and optional light activity data.

TABLE 2
Insight Gen Z and younger millennials are wellness-aware, but wellness is not only fitness. It is sleep, mood, movement, food, sunlight, routine, connection, and small daily actions that make people feel more grounded.

TABLE 3
Short pitch | Longer pitch
Real-Life Challenges turn small healthy actions into shared BeReal moments. | Real-Life Challenges help close friends create playful, human rituals around movement, mood, care, and connection. The feature gives users more reasons to open BeReal without changing the app into a performance-based social network.

TABLE 4
Avoid | Aim for
Track your habits. | Show me one real thing you did today.
Beat your friends. | Let us both show up today.
Complete your workout. | Send proof you went outside.
Optimize your life. | Make one small real-life promise.
Win the leaderboard. | Enjoy the journey together.

TABLE 5
Element | Description
Person | Choose a best friend, partner, sibling, or small group.
Action | Select one real-life action to complete.
Rhythm | Daily, weekly, three times a week, weekend-only, or one-time.
Proof | Photo, dual-camera photo, short video, voice note, mood word, check-in, or optional light activity data.
Response | RealMoji, short reply, voice reply, or “I did mine too.”
Memory | A small recap showing how both people showed up over time.

TABLE 6
Family | Purpose | Example prompts
Body / Movement | Movement without performance pressure. | 10-minute walk; stretch once today; sunlight walk; post-workout Real; dance to one song; stand-up break.
Mind / Mood | Small emotional check-ins without becoming clinical. | Mood compass; one thing I survived today; before/after walk mood; name the feeling; quiet five minutes; one-sentence journal.
Care / Daily Life | Small actions that make the day more stable. | Drink water; real meal; clean one corner; make your bed; sleep wind-down; medication or vitamin check, if private.
Connection / Relationship | Relationship-based rituals that feel native to BeReal. | Same sky photo; send a real voice note; best friend proof-of-life; coffee together or apart; one honest question; partner check-in.
World / Outside | Prompts that move users out of isolated scrolling. | Touch grass; sit outside; notice one beautiful thing; park moment; library or cafe moment; plant, pet, or sky check.

TABLE 7
Data type | Examples | Risk | Recommendation
Expressive data | Photo, video, caption, RealMoji, voice note | Low to medium | Core of the feature.
Light wellness data | Steps, active minutes, standing hours, hydration check, sleep wind-down check | Medium | Optional and rounded by default.
Sensitive data | Mood logs, medication, sleep quality, heart rate, precise location | High | Opt-in only, private, minimal, and never required.

TABLE 9
Widget message | Purpose

TABLE 10
Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation
It becomes Strava | Too much focus on steps, rankings, and performance could break BeReal’s tone. | Make visual proof and relationship rituals the center.
It becomes fake therapy | Mental health language can create false expectations. | Frame as social wellbeing, not treatment.
Users feel shame when they miss a day | Streaks and challenges can create guilt. | Use mercy days, gentle reset, and private progress.
Privacy concerns | Health, mood, and location data can be sensitive. | Use opt-in controls, rounded data, and small audiences.
Too many prompts | A large library can feel noisy. | Start with curated families and a small MVP set.
Performative wellness | Users may stage wellness moments for social approval. | Keep groups small and use mutual reveal.
Safety and context | Outside or movement prompts may not be safe for everyone. | Offer indoor alternatives and customization.

TABLE 11
MVP Launch with 12 curated challenges across four categories: Move, Mood, Care, and Connect. Use only three proof types at first: photo/dual-camera, voice note, and simple check-in. Add health data, widget integration, custom pacts, and recaps in later versions.
