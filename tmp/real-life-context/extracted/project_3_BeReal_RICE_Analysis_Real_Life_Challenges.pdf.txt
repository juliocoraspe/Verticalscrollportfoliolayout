Selection rationale
Why not the top-scoring idea? Clarity Layer has the highest RICE score, but changing onboarding and UI explanations may challenge choices
that BeReal has likely made deliberately. It is useful, but it may be more of a UX refinement than a strong product direction.
Why not the widget? The widget is still a strong idea, but by itself it can feel like adapting another app pattern. It reduces friction, but it needs a
deeper BeReal-native purpose to feel original.
Why Real-Life Challenges? Real-Life Challenges creates a new space for BeReal without leaving its philosophy. It turns small actions like
walking, checking in, touching grass, sending a voice note, or sharing a mood into playful rituals with people who matter.
Strategic fit. This feature can appeal to Gen Z wellness behaviors while also giving millennials a clearer and more personal reason to use
BeReal. It supports engagement, close-friend connection, and authentic sharing without becoming a performance-based fitness network.
Evidence considered

--- PAGE 2 ---
- BeReal says it has over 40 million monthly active users and an audience that is 85% Gen Z, which makes Gen Z behavior important for
prioritization.
- McKinsey reports that Gen Z and millennials increasingly treat wellness as a daily, personalized practice, supporting the idea of lightweight
wellness rituals.
- Gallup reports that young adults have become less likely to drink alcohol than young adults in prior decades, which supports a broader shift
toward more intentional health behavior.
- Research on BeReal suggests that spontaneity, reciprocity, and curated audiences can reduce some self-presentation pressure, but the app can
also feel limited. Real-Life Challenges aims to expand expression without adding a performative feed.
Sources: BeReal U.S. Advertising announcement, McKinsey Future of Wellness 2025, Gallup young adults drinking trends, and
ACM/ArXiv research on BeReal and authentic self-presentation.
