# NARRATIVE ARC — Real-Life Challenges Presentation
*How to tell the story so Ben gets it*

---

## 🎭 THE STORY YOU'RE TELLING

**Opening Hook (10 seconds)**
```
"BeReal peaked at 73.5 million users in 2022. 
Today it's 16 million. 

The demographic that should be helping them grow — millennials — 
are actually leaving the fastest."
```

**Why this works:** Data + tension. Ben immediately knows this is about a real problem.

---

## 📍 ACT 1: THE PROBLEM (The Diagnosis)
**Duration:** 45 seconds | **Goal:** Make Ben care about millennials

### Opening Insight
```
"You'd think millennials would love BeReal. They invented authentic sharing. 
But BeReal is only 8% millennials, while 38% of the social media world is millennials.

That's a gap."
```

### The Root Cause
```
"It's not that they don't like BeReal. It's that they're CONFUSED by it.

The research from Project 1 shows millennials experience:
- Cognitive overload (too many mechanics)
- Mechanic opacity (what even is a streak? why am I locked out?)

And because it's confusing, they churn.
Because they churn, BeReal loses its only underserved demographic."
```

### The Stakes
```
"This isn't just a retention problem. It's a revenue problem.

If BeReal only appeals to Gen Z, RealBrands can only sell Gen Z advertiser campaigns. 
Millennials expand the advertiser base. That's why this matters."
```

### Transition
```
"So the question becomes: 
How do we give millennials a reason to RETURN to BeReal 
while also giving them CLARITY on what to do?"
```

---

## 💡 ACT 2: THE SOLUTION (The Diagnosis)
**Duration:** 60 seconds | **Goal:** Make Ben see the elegance of the idea

### The Insight
```
"What if we didn't fix the confusion by explaining the app better?

What if we gave users a clear, specific REASON to open it?

Not 'open BeReal to see posts.' 
But 'open BeReal to do THIS with someone you care about.'"
```

### The Concept (with examples)
```
"Real-Life Challenges is simple:

You and a friend make a small pact. Drink water today. Take a walk. 
Send a voice note. Touch grass. 

You each capture it the way BeReal already captures life — 
with a photo, a voice note, a video, or a simple check-in.

Then you see each other's moment. That's it. No scores. No leaderboard. 
No shame if you skip a day."
```

### Why It's NOT What You Think
```
"This is not a fitness app. McKinsey research shows millennials 
want wellness, but they DON'T want another fitness app.

This is five categories:
- Body/Movement (walk, stretch, sunlight)
- Mind/Mood (mood check, journal, name your feeling)
- Care/Daily Life (water, meal, clean one corner)
- Connection (voice note, honest question, same-sky photo)
- World/Outside (touch grass, sit at a café, park moment)

It's about rituals. Not performance."
```

### The Loop
```
"The beauty of it is the loop:

1. Make a challenge (you pick the action)
2. Show up (do the real thing)
3. See them (post proof BeReal-style)
4. Recap (quiet, no flex)

Then you either continue or make a new one with someone else.

It's closed. It's intentional. It's small."
```

### Transition
```
"But here's the thing: I didn't just pick this idea randomly. 
Let me show you why this was the RIGHT choice among seven options."
```

---

## 📊 ACT 3: THE VALIDATION (The Case)
**Duration:** 75 seconds | **Goal:** Make Ben believe this is strategically sound

### The RICE Analysis
```
"I evaluated seven strategic features using the RICE framework:
Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort = Score

Here's what I found:"
```

**Show the table quickly:**
```
1. Clarity Layer (onboarding fix)        → RICE 85
2. BeReal Widget (home screen widget)    → RICE 48
3. Real-Life Challenges                  → RICE 30 ← CHOSEN
4. Shared Moments                        → RICE 25
5-7. [Others]                            → Lower scores
```

### Why NOT the Top Score?
```
"Clarity Layer has the highest RICE (85), right?

But changing BeReal's onboarding and UI explanations 
risks conflicting with choices BeReal made deliberately.

It's useful, but it's a UX refinement, not a product direction."
```

### Why NOT the Widget?
```
"The Widget (RICE 48) is still strong.

But by itself, it feels like we're copying another app's pattern.
It reduces friction, but it needs NATIVE PURPOSE.

Real-Life Challenges gives it that purpose."
```

### Why YES Real-Life Challenges?
```
"Real-Life Challenges (RICE 30) does something different:

It creates NEW SPACE inside BeReal without leaving its philosophy.

✓ It solves the millennial retention problem (CLARITY + CONNECTION)
✓ It solves the Gen Z engagement problem (wellness + authenticity + close-friends)
✓ It MAINTAINS BeReal's core values:
   - Authenticity (proof is real, no filters)
   - Privacy (close-friends only, data is optional)
   - Simplicity (6 screens, few taps)
   - Positive behavior (connection, not shame)

That alignment is why it won."
```

### Transition
```
"Now let me show you what this actually looks like in the prototype."
```

---

## 🎨 ACT 4: THE PROTOTYPE (The Proof)
**Duration:** 90 seconds | **Goal:** Make it REAL and CONCRETE

### Framing
```
"The MVP is 6 screens. Each one does one thing clearly.

I'll walk you through a real user flow: 
Someone setting up a 'drink water' challenge with a friend."
```

### Screen by Screen Narrative

#### Screen 01 (Empty State)
```
[Show screen]

"First time someone opens Real-Life Challenges, they see an empty card.
No pressure, no examples pushing them toward something.

Just: 'No challenge yet. Start something small with someone close.'"
```

#### Screen 02 (Family Selection)
```
[Show screen]

"They tap 'Start a challenge' and see five families.
Body/Movement glows yellow when selected.
One simple example per family — 'Drink water today.'

This is where clarity happens. They see EXACTLY what they're choosing."
```

#### Screen 03 (Setup & Invite)
```
[Show screen]

"Next screen: They pick the action (water), the person (Maya), 
the rhythm (3 days), and the proof type (photo).

Everything is visible. Nothing is hidden. Then they send the invite."
```

#### Screen 04 (First-Day Onboarding)
```
[Show screen]

"When the challenge starts, they get a bridge card:
'You're in. Maya too.'

Then three lines:
1. Tap Start Day 1. The camera opens.
2. Drink your water.
3. Hit capture. Day 1 done.

This is the tone of the whole feature. Conversational. Chatty. 
Human. ZERO jargon."
```

#### Screen 05 (Success & Reveal)
```
[Show screen]

"They post their moment. 
They see: 'Nice. Day 1 is done. Saved to your recap.'

Light yellow banner — not confetti, not celebration, just:
'You did it. Let's move on.'

Then they can tap to see how Maya showed up."
```

#### Screen 06 (Repeat/Lockscreen)
```
[Show screen]

"For Day 2 and beyond, they see it on their lockscreen:
'Day 2 of 3. You up?'

With a chatty notification:
'psst — Maya just posted Day 2. Sneak yours in?'

No generic app language. Just a friend saying: you next?"
```

### The Tone is Everything
```
"Notice the copy never says:
- 'You missed a day' (no shame)
- 'Streak: 2' (no gamification)
- 'View Leaderboard' (no competition)
- 'Great job!' (no false celebration)

It says:
- 'You up?'
- 'Maya drank her water'
- 'Psst — your turn?'

That tone difference is what makes this feel like a BeReal feature, 
not a fitness app feature."
```

### Transition
```
"So the prototype is ready. Now here's how we know if it works."
```

---

## ✅ ACT 5: THE METRICS (The Plan)
**Duration:** 30 seconds | **Goal:** Show he has thought about success

### Primary Metric
```
"The main thing we measure: 
WEEKLY ACTIVE PARTICIPATION in Real-Life Challenges.

Why? Because it tells us if users have a new, meaningful reason to return 
during the month. Not just when the daily BeReal notification pings."
```

### Secondary Metrics (quick list)
```
- Challenge creation rate (do they understand it?)
- Challenge completion rate (do they actually do it?)
- Posts per user per week (new posting behavior?)
- Friend response rate (does it create connection?)
- 30-day retention (challenge users vs non-challenge?)
- Repeat challenge rate (do they want to keep doing it?)
```

### Transition
```
"Those metrics tell us if we're solving the millennial retention problem. 
And that's the ultimate goal here."
```

---

## 🔑 ACT 6: THE QUESTIONS (Anticipation)
**Duration:** Variable | **Goal:** Be ready for what Ben will ask

### Q: "How is this different from a fitness app?"
```
ANSWER:
"Fitness apps measure performance. This doesn't.

No leaderboard. No scores. No streaks. No rankings.

You're not trying to beat Maya. You're trying to DO it with Maya.

The proof is real (photo, voice, check-in) — not data from your Apple Watch.

And the categories include mood, connection, care — 
not just movement. So it's about rituals, not fitness."
```

### Q: "What if no one participates?"
```
ANSWER:
"That's why the feature is optional.

You only see it if you want to create a challenge. 
No FOMO, no default pressure.

And BeReal has the close-friends mechanic built in. 
This just gives that mechanic a PURPOSE.

If millennials don't want it, we learn that. But I think they will, 
because the research shows they want connection + intention, 
not another tracking app."
```

### Q: "Why does this help millennials specifically?"
```
ANSWER:
"Three things:

1. CLARITY: Millennials were confused. This gives them a specific reason 
   to open: 'Do this challenge with me today.'

2. CONNECTION: Millennials feel the app is lonely. This makes it about 
   'two people showing up,' not 'me posting to a feed.'

3. AUTHENTICITY: Millennials don't want performance pressure. This has 
   no scores, no shame, no leaderboard — just small rituals with people 
   they care about.

The RICE analysis showed this feature moves the needle on retention 
for that demographic while maintaining BeReal values."
```

### Q: "Could this work for Gen Z too?"
```
ANSWER:
"Yes, actually.

Gen Z is into wellness and mental health (McKinsey research). 
They like close-friend connection (that's why BeReal succeeded with them).

So Real-Life Challenges appeals to both:
- Millennials: clarity + intention
- Gen Z: wellness + close-friends + authenticity

The feature isn't built FOR millennials. It's built to solve 
millennial retention WHILE also engaging Gen Z further."
```

---

## 🎬 ACT 7: THE CLOSE (The Call)
**Duration:** 30 seconds | **Goal:** Leave him with ONE clear idea

### The Reframe
```
"Here's the thing about BeReal's decline:

It's not that the app is broken. It's that it's incomplete.

It captures one spontaneous moment a day beautifully.

What it doesn't do yet is help two people show up for something together.

Real-Life Challenges finishes that thought."
```

### The 1-Sentence Summary
```
"It expands BeReal from a daily snapshot 
into small, shared rituals — giving millennials clarity and purpose 
while keeping the authenticity that makes BeReal special."
```

### The 3 Takeaways
```
1. Solves millennial retention: 8% of MAU today, 38% of market globally
2. Gives new reason to open: Beyond the daily post
3. Maintains BeReal values: Authentic, private, simple, positive
```

### The Ask
```
"The prototypeready to test with millennials. 

If we see weekly participation go up and 30-day retention lift 
for challenge users, we know we have something.

Let's find out."
```

### Final Thought
```
[Pause. Look at Ben.]

"This is about giving the platform a second reason to exist.
Not a second app. One deeper version of the same app.

That's the opportunity here."
```

## ⏰ PACING GUIDE

Use this to know where you are in your timing:

```
0:00-0:10   Opening Hook (problem in 10 sec)
0:10-0:55   ACT 1: Problem (45 sec)
0:55-1:55   ACT 2: Solution (60 sec)
1:55-3:10   ACT 3: Validation (75 sec)
3:10-4:40   ACT 4: Prototype (90 sec)
4:40-5:10   ACT 5: Metrics (30 sec)
5:10-5:40   ACT 6: Close (30 sec)
5:40+       Q&A (as long as it takes)
```

**Ideal landing: 5:40 - 6:00 total. Ben asks questions. You're done in 6-7 min.**

---

## 🎯 TONE THROUGHOUT

✓ **Confident but not cocky** — You've done the research. Show it.
✓ **Curious not prescriptive** — "Let's find out" not "This will definitely work"
✓ **Specific not vague** — Always have data, examples, numbers
✓ **Human not robotic** — Say "Maya" not "the user," "drink water" not "hydration"
✓ **Story not bullet points** — Flow from problem → solution → why → proof

---

## 💪 POWER MOVE (If you want to land this)

After you close, PAUSE for 3 seconds. 

Let Ben sit with the idea.

Then say: "What do you want to dig into?"

This does two things:
1. Shows confidence (you're not rushing to fill silence)
2. Puts him in control (he gets to guide the conversation)
3. Makes it a dialogue, not a pitch
